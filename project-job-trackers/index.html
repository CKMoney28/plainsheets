Gantt chart, task tracker or job costing: which do you need?

A task list answers “what's left and who's doing it”. A Gantt chart adds time, so you can see what overlaps and what a late start pushes back; it's most useful when one task can't start until another finishes. A project tracker sits in between and is the better pick when you run several small projects at once. Job costing is a different question: whether a job is making money. If you quote fixed prices, log costs against the job as they happen, not at the end, and include costs you've committed to but haven't paid yet. For pricing a job before you win it, see our quotes & estimates templates. A spreadsheet starts to strain when several people update the same plan at once or you need reminders, so keep it to one owner per file where you can.

Gantt chart template

by Vertex42
ExcelFree

The classic Excel Gantt chart. Each row has a WBS number, task, lead, predecessor, start date, days, % done and work days, and the bars draw across a dated timeline; a Display Week setting moves the view along a longer project. Task category rows group the work into phases. A good fit if you need something you can print and hand to a client or crew.

Catch: No email. The download page asks you to mark Vertex42 as a preferred Google source, and you can skip that. One tab advertises the paid Pro version. Excel and OpenOffice files only, no Google Sheets copy. Private-use license, so you can't redistribute or resell it.

Gantt chart template for Excel

by gantt-chart.io
ExcelFree

One plan sheet and a How to use sheet. Each task has a phase, owner, start, duration in days and progress; the end date is a formula and a 26-week grid colours the weeks each task covers, with the finished part of the bar shaded darker. The how-to explains chaining tasks with a formula like =F7+1, so moving one task moves everything after it.

Catch: Direct .xlsx with no email or sign-up, and no macros. There's also a CSV. The publisher sells an online Gantt chart app and promotes it on the page.

Project tracker template

by Excel TV
ExcelFree

For someone juggling several projects at once. Job cost tracker

by Werx
ExcelFree

A one-page job costing sheet for contractors. Enter the contract amount and a budget for labor, materials, subcontractors, equipment, permits and other costs, then fill in committed and actual costs as they come in. Variance per line and estimated profit (contract minus actual) calculate for you. The committed column is for purchase orders and sub contracts you've signed but haven't been billed for yet.

Catch: Direct .xlsx with no email; there's also a print-ready PDF. Clicking download pops up a Werx box advertising a free trial of its field-service software, which you can close. One job per file.

by OpenSheets
