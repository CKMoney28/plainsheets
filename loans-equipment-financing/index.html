Amortization schedules, lease vs buy, credit lines and DSCR: which one do you need?

An amortization schedule splits each payment on a term loan into interest and principal and shows the balance after every payment, so you can see what the loan really costs and what paying extra would save. A balloon loan has smaller payments and a large amount due at the end; the calculator shows how large, so you can plan for it. Before you sign, compare offers with fees included, not just the rate or the monthly payment, and for equipment, weigh leasing against buying over the same number of years. A line of credit has no fixed schedule, so the tracker's job is to show what you owe right now. When you apply, lenders usually ask for a business debt schedule (everything you already owe) and look at your debt service coverage ratio (DSCR), your yearly cash flow divided by your yearly loan payments. These sheets estimate and record; they aren't a loan offer or financial, tax or legal advice, and your lender's figures win if they differ. Loan amortization schedule with extra payments

by Vertex42
ExcelGoogle SheetsFree

Enter the loan amount, annual rate, term in years, first payment date and how often you pay (annual through weekly), and it builds the full payment schedule, rounded to the cent, with the last payment adjusted so the balance ends at zero. An extra-payment column lets you try paying more, or a one-off lump sum, and shows the estimated interest saved. A yearly summary adds up interest and principal for each year, and two more tabs let you log actual payments as you make them.

Catch: No sign-up; the download page leads to a direct .xlsx, plus a Google Sheets version. Personal-use license (Vertex42 sells a commercial version for lenders and advisers). The schedule sheet is protected without a password to prevent accidental edits. Fixed-rate loans only; if your interest accrues daily by payment date, Vertex42 points to its separate simple-interest loan sheet. The sample starts in 2021.

by The Margin Sheet
ExcelFree

Yellow cells for the loan amount, rate, term, payments per year (12, 4, 2 or 1), first payment date and an extra payment per period. by Business Plan Download

Balloon loan calculator

by Vertex42
ExcelGoogle SheetsFree

For a loan with smaller regular payments and a large final payment: enter the amount, rate, the amortization period that sets the payment and the number of payments before the balloon. It shows the monthly payment, the balloon payment and total interest, with a monthly schedule, and has an interest-only option. In the sample, $20,000 at 7.5% amortized over 36 months with the balance due at month 12 gives payments of $622.12 and a balloon of $14,447.28.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx and a Google Sheets version. Personal-use license. It assumes monthly compounding and month-end payments, up to 360 months. The sample date is from 2015.

Loan offer comparison worksheet

by LoansFlow
ExcelFree

Up to four quotes side by side: amount, rate, term, upfront fee as a percent and other fees in dollars. For each it works out the monthly payment, total payments, total interest, total fees, total cost of borrowing, the cash you actually receive after fees and an estimated yearly cost that includes the fees (an APR-style figure you can hold against each lender's stated APR), then names the offer with the lowest total cost.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx. The same page has a DSCR worksheet and an SBA 7(a) application checklist. The "lowest total cost" line compares dollars across different terms, so a short loan usually wins even when its payment is much higher; in the sample it picks a 3-year offer whose payment is more than twice the 10-year one. Check the payment against your cash flow too. Prepayment penalties are a notes row only. LoansFlow is a loan information site, not a lender.

Lease vs buy analysis

by WordNExcelTemplates
ExcelFree

For equipment or a vehicle. Buy side: purchase price, yearly maintenance and resale value at the end. Lease side: monthly payment, upfront fee and any maintenance you pay. Add the term (up to 10 years) and your discount rate, and it lays out the cash cost year by year, totals both options in cash and in present value, and says which is cheaper and by how much. In the sample, buying costs less in cash ($21,600 vs $25,460) but leasing is $834.92 cheaper in present value.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx, one of five purchase-decision sheets on the page (the others cover payback and ROI, vendor quotes, capital purchase requests and software costs). Buying is treated as paying cash up front, with no loan; lease payments are treated as paid at each year end; tax effects aren't included, so ask your accountant how each option is deducted.

Business debt schedule

by Duneland Financial
ExcelPDFFree

The list lenders ask for when you apply. Up to 12 obligations with creditor, debt type (dropdown), original date and amount, current balance, rate, maturity and credit-line limit; each payment and its frequency is converted to a monthly equivalent and totaled. You enter the balance-sheet total for these debts and it shows any difference, and cells say "n.a." when required information is missing. An optional section records proposed payoffs at a closing without changing today's totals. A filled fictional example is included.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx and a PDF version. Formula cells are protected without a password. Built by a business financing firm for loan applications and acquisitions; you don't need its services to use the file. Monthly equivalents are simple arithmetic, not an amortization forecast, and your lender may want its own form.

DSCR loan calculator

by ArumSoft
ExcelFree

Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) is the cash your business produces divided by its yearly loan payments. Enter net income, add-backs on their own tab (depreciation, existing interest, one-time items), existing annual debt service and the proposed loan; it shows the new payment, DSCR against your lender's minimum (1.25 by default), the cushion, and the largest payment and loan your cash flow supports. Extra tabs show DSCR by year, a grid by loan amount and rate, and a checks list. The sample: $125,500 of cash flow and a $450,000 loan at 8.75% over 10 years gives 1.30.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx (22 KB). Business cash flow only; it doesn't include the household side some lenders check for owner-guaranteed loans. The amortization table goes up to 25 years. Lenders each calculate DSCR their own way, so treat it as an estimate, not an approval.
