Statement or ledger: which do you need?

A profit & loss statement (also called an income statement) is a summary: income minus expenses for a month, quarter or year. A bookkeeping ledger is the list of every payment in and out that the summary comes from. If you already have totals from your bank or card statements, a statement template is enough. If you're starting from scratch, a ledger is less work over a year because the totals fill in as you go. Most very small businesses keep cash-basis books, recording income when money arrives and expenses when they pay. Transfers between your own accounts, owner draws, loan principal and credit card payments don't belong on a P&L. These templates are record-keeping tools, not tax or accounting advice, so check anything you file with a tax professional.

12-month profit & loss statement

by ForProfit.io
ExcelSheetsFree

Twelve monthly columns plus a total, with expense rows laid out to match IRS Schedule C. Subtotals and net profit fill in as you type. There's a blank tab and a filled-in example tab, and the page walks through filling it from your bank statements on a cash basis.

Catch: ForProfit.io sells a paid service that builds P&L reports from uploaded bank statements, and the site pushes a free trial. The template itself is a direct .xlsx download with no email or sign-up. For Sheets, use File, Import, Upload.
