Markup, margin or break-even: which do you need?

Markup is profit as a share of your cost; margin is profit as a share of the price. They aren't the same number: a 50% markup on a $10 item gives a $15 price and a 33% margin, not 50%. If you set a target margin, price with cost ÷ (1 − margin), which most of these files do for you. Break-even answers a different question: how many sales cover your fixed costs (rent, software, insurance) at the price you've picked. Run both. A healthy-looking margin can still leave you short if volume is low, and every discount raises the number of sales you need. Service businesses should start from an hourly rate that covers unbilled time, and food businesses from a costed recipe. Break-even analysis template

by Smartsheet
ExcelFree

A one-sheet break-even worksheet that splits variable costs into per-unit dollar costs (cost of goods, labor, overhead) and percentage costs such as commissions, next to a list of fixed cost items. Enter your selling price and it works out break-even units and break-even sales. Good if your costs include a cut of each sale, not just a fixed amount per unit.

Catch: No email; the .xlsx downloads straight from the page. The page around it promotes Smartsheet's paid work-management software, which you don't need. Excel only, with a disclaimer tab. The sample cells are blank placeholders, so there's no worked example to compare against.

Break-even calculator (UK)

by The Business Kit
ExcelFree

Inputs for period, fixed costs, selling price, variable cost per unit, a target profit and your capacity. It returns contribution per unit, contribution margin, break-even units and revenue, the units you need to hit the target profit, and a capacity check that warns when break-even is more than you can actually make. A scenarios table compares different cost and price combinations side by side.

Catch: No email or account. Built for UK businesses, so money is labelled in pounds (£) and prices are entered excluding VAT; change the labels if you're in the US. One sheet plus a PDF field guide.

Pricing calculator

by Business Plan Download
ExcelFree

The simplest file here: one sheet, one row per product or service. Type the full cost per unit and the margin you want, and it suggests a price, shows the equivalent markup and profit per unit, and puts your competitors' low and high prices next to it before you settle on a final price and see its actual margin.

Catch: No email. The page says it opens in Google Sheets and LibreOffice too; you'd upload the .xlsx, as there's no copy link. Rows for about ten products; add more by copying a row down. The site also promotes its own business plan builder.

Product pricing calculator

by Spreadsheet Partners
ExcelFree

Builds each product's cost from components on a Costs tab, then shows cost, price, margin and markup on the Pricing tab. Its most useful column is the break-even volume for a discount: how many extra units you'd need to sell to make the same profit after a 10% (or any) discount. A Scenarios tab runs several discount levels against your first product.

Catch: No email or account, and the page says it's free for commercial use. It's an .xlsx the page says works in Google Sheets; upload it to Drive, as there's no copy link. The sample products are digital goods, so swap in your own.

Profit margin calculator

by SheetFor
ExcelFree

A product list with unit cost, price and units sold a month, which fills in gross profit per item, margin, markup, the price you'd need to hit a target margin, and monthly gross profit, for up to 200 products. A small Break-even tab takes your monthly fixed costs and shows profit after them. The candle shop sample suits Etsy and craft sellers.

Markup and margin pricing sheet

by MFG Calcs
ExcelFree

Aimed at manufacturers and job shops that price from cost. One row per product or quote line with unit cost, markup, target margin and quantity; it shows the price your markup gives, the margin that price really makes, the price your target margin needs, the gap between them and a status in words. A dashboard adds a markup-to-margin conversion table, and a Verify tab re-checks the formulas.

Catch: No email, no macros. It comes with a CSV and a PDF how-to; you only need the .xlsx. The example is a fictional machine shop, so there are more tabs than a small shop needs.

Charge-out rate spreadsheet (UK)

by Joqiva
ExcelFree

For sole traders and consultants who sell their time. List your business costs (with their frequency), set working weeks, days, hours and non-billable time, and enter the income you want; it works out the revenue you need, your billable hours, and an hourly and day rate. Enter your current rate to see the surplus or shortfall, and scenarios show the rate at a few more or fewer billable hours a week.

Catch: No email or registration. UK-focused, so amounts are in pounds (£) and exclude VAT; rates are before personal tax. There's a blank template and a filled-in example to download. Joqiva sells software for service businesses, which you don't need.

Recipe costing template

by Scandallo
ExcelFree

For restaurants, cafés and food trucks. Cost one dish ingredient by ingredient, with portions, VAT and your target food cost percentage, and get the recipe cost, cost per portion, a suggested price and the gross profit per portion.
