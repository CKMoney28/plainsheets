Maintenance log, schedule or asset register?

They answer different questions. A maintenance log looks back: what was done to this machine, when, at what hour reading and for how much. It's what you show for a warranty claim or when you sell the equipment. A maintenance schedule looks forward: what's due next and what's overdue, which is where breakdowns are prevented. An asset register is the list of everything the business owns, with what it cost, where it is and who has it, and sometimes its depreciated value. Most small shops need a schedule and a log first; the register matters once you have more equipment than you can remember or your accountant asks for one. Use the same asset ID in all three so they line up. The depreciation in these sheets is straight-line "book" depreciation for your own records; tax depreciation follows different rules, so check with your tax preparer. For stock you sell rather than equipment you use, see our inventory templates; to log business miles for taxes, see mileage & expense trackers.

Equipment maintenance log (one machine)

by Tenmil
ExcelFree

One workbook per machine. You fill in the unit number, make and model, serial number and site at the top, then log each job: date, hour-meter reading, type (PM, repair, inspection or other from a dropdown), work performed, parts used, labor hours and labor, parts and other costs. Each row totals itself, and a summary block works out total maintenance cost, total labor hours, hours run between the first and last entry, and maintenance cost per hour. There are 60 rows plus a totals row, and a printable PDF version is on the same page.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx download. It's one machine per file, so for a fleet you'll be keeping several copies. There are example rows to clear before you start. Tenmil sells maintenance software, and the page mentions it.

Equipment maintenance log

by Sockeye
ExcelGoogle SheetsFree

A one-page record for a single piece of equipment: name, serial number, location, purchase and manufacture dates and the manufacturer's contact details at the top, then a service table (date, maintenance type, who did it, what was done, labor plus parts cost, next service due) and a notes table for warranty details and recurring problems. Maintenance types and technician names are dropdowns you can edit. It comes with a how-to tab and a filled-in example tab.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx or a Google Sheets "make a copy" link (sign in to Google). The service table only has three rows, so you'll need to insert more almost straight away. There are no formulas: the cost column doesn't total and the next due date is typed in. The blog post around it promotes Sockeye's maintenance software.

Vehicle maintenance log with fleet inventory

by Vertex42
ExcelGoogle SheetsFree

A service log for a vehicle: make, model, year, VIN and engine at the top, then date, mileage at service, work performed, who did it, cost, invoice or receipt number and notes, with a running total cost. Duplicate the sheet for each vehicle; a separate Fleet Inventory sheet lists every vehicle with its fleet ID, purchase date and cost, who it's assigned to, condition and maintenance cost. There's a link to look up the manufacturer's recommended service schedule.

Catch: No email; the .xlsx comes from a "your download is ready" page after one extra click, and there's a Google Sheets version too. Vertex42's license is for private use, so don't resell or share modified copies. The fleet inventory's maintenance cost column doesn't pull from the vehicle logs by itself; the sheet tells you to add formulas to link them. Equipment calibration log

by Vertex42
ExcelGoogle SheetsFree

A table for tools and instruments that need calibrating on a schedule: name, description, ID tag, department and room, the date it was last calibrated and how many days until the next one. The next calibration date is worked out for you, and conditional formatting highlights overdue dates. A help tab explains sorting the list by next calibration date. Handy for scales, gauges, torque wrenches and test meters.

Catch: No email; .xlsx after one extra click, or a Google Sheets version. Vertex42's private-use license applies. It only tracks dates, with no column for calibration results, certificates or who did the work, so add those yourself if an auditor will ask.

Preventive maintenance (PM) schedule

by Maintainful
ExcelFree

One row per maintenance task per asset: asset, location, task, who it's assigned to, a calendar interval (every so many days, weeks, months or years) and/or a meter interval (run hours, kilometers, cycles). Enter when it was last done and the sheet works out the next due date and next service reading, then shows Overdue, Due soon or OK, whichever interval comes first. There's room for about 200 tasks, an estimated-minutes column, example rows and a how-to tab. A printable 12-month planner PDF is also offered.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx. The status uses TODAY(), so it changes every day; that's the point, but it means a printed copy is only right on the day you print it. Grey columns are formulas, so don't type over them. You have to keep the current meter readings up to date yourself for hour-based jobs. The file mentions Maintainful's software.

Machine maintenance schedule

by Smartsheet
ExcelGoogle SheetsFree

A simple equipment list with machine name, condition (good, fair, poor or bad from a dropdown, color-coded), location, who it's assigned to, last maintenance date, maintenance frequency in days and notes. The next maintenance date is calculated from the last date plus the frequency. The same page also offers kitchen, office, gym and heavy-equipment maintenance schedules and an equipment inventory with a depreciation schedule, if one of those fits better.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx or a Google Sheets link (make your own copy). It doesn't flag overdue dates by itself; you have to sort by next date and look. There's a "create in Smartsheet" banner in the file. Smartsheet's disclaimer says the templates are for reference only.

Asset tracking template

by Smartsheet
ExcelGoogle SheetsFree

A register of what the business owns: item number, name, description, type, department, room, date of last order, vendor, purchase price, warranty expiry date, condition, quantity, model, vendor number, remarks and a column for a photo or link. Total value per row (price × quantity) and a total asset value at the top are calculated. It includes a filled-in example tab, a blank tab and an asset vendor list with contacts and lead times.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx or a Google Sheets link. It records what you paid, not what things are worth now, so there's no depreciation. The example tab has a typo or two in the sample data. The same page has an IT asset tracker and a simple asset management sheet if you mostly track laptops and phones.

Fixed asset tracker with depreciation schedule

by Smartsheet
ExcelGoogle SheetsFree

One row per asset: serial number, condition, description, who it's assigned to, vendor, whether a bank loan is attached, purchase date, expected life, purchase price and expected end-of-life value. The sheet works out the asset's end date, months until replacement, an alert date three months before the end, annual and monthly straight-line depreciation, and today's estimated value. It has an example tab and a blank tab.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx or a Google Sheets link. Current value is based on TODAY() and doesn't stop at the end-of-life value, so for assets past their expected life it keeps falling, even below zero; check old rows by hand. It works out the end date as 365 days a year, so it can be a day or two out after leap years. The sample purchase date is in 2028, so clear the examples first. This is book depreciation only, not tax depreciation.

Fixed asset register and depreciation template

by CloseFlow
ExcelFree

An accountant-style register for 50 assets: asset ID, category, in-service date, cost, salvage value and useful life, with monthly and annual straight-line depreciation, status, disposal date and proceeds, accumulated depreciation, net book value and gain or loss on disposal. Set a month-end date and it builds a monthly roll-forward plus ready-made depreciation and disposal journal entries with a "Balanced?" check. A start tab explains every step, and there's a useful-life guide by category.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx. Formula cells are locked (no password; Review → Unprotect Sheet to change them). Book depreciation only: the page says it doesn't do MACRS, bonus depreciation or Section 179, so it won't match your tax return. CloseFlow sells a QuickBooks add-on and promotes it on the start tab. Probably more than you need if you only own a handful of things.

Fixed asset register (Kenya-made)

by STL Asset Solutions
ExcelFree

A 22-column register with room for 500 assets: asset number, description, serial number, category, location, department, custodian, condition, status, acquisition date and cost, residual value, depreciation method (straight line or reducing balance), useful life, years held, accumulated depreciation and net book value, plus tag type and disposal details. Useful life fills in from the category, and a summary tab totals cost, depreciation and book value by category, leaving out disposed assets. A read-me tab is honest about what a spreadsheet can't do.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx. It was made for Kenyan businesses, so money columns are labeled KSh; relabel them for dollars (the formulas don't care). Years held uses TODAY(), so the values move every day. The default useful lives are illustrative, not IRS lives. The read-me tab advertises STL's tagging services in Kenya.
