Bills, subscriptions and petty cash: what goes where?

A bill tracker (accountants call it accounts payable) lists what you owe suppliers and when it's due; the useful habit is entering each bill the day it arrives, not the day you pay it, and checking the list once a week. Subscriptions deserve their own list because they renew quietly on a card: note the renewal date, the notice period, and who actually uses each tool, then decide before the cancel-by date rather than after the charge. Petty cash works best as a fixed float, say $200: every payment out gets a slip, and when you top up, you put back exactly what was spent, so the slips plus the cash always add up to the float. A cash drawer count is the end-of-day version for a till: count the cash, compare it with what the register says, and note any difference with a reason. These sheets are records; they don't pay bills or send reminders. Accounts payable tracker with weekly payment schedule

by BookkeepingSheets
ExcelFree

Room for 150 bills. Enter the vendor, bill number, bill date, payment terms in days, amount and anything already paid, and the sheet works out the due date, balance, days until due and a status (Overdue, Due soon, Upcoming or Paid). If a supplier offers an early-payment discount such as 2/10 net 30, it also shows the discount deadline, what you'd save and the date to pay by. The AP Report tab gives six headline numbers, a week-by-week payment schedule, open balances by vendor and the next 15 bills to pay.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx download. The report date on the Start Here tab is a fixed sample date, so type =TODAY() there or the statuses won't move. Eleven example bills to delete first. The page also has an online version with an optional account; you don't need it to use the file.

Accounts payable / bills tracker with dashboard

by Office Templates Online
ExcelFree

A workbook with a Bills log (vendor and category dropdowns, amount, amount paid, balance due), a Payment Log with a payment-method dropdown, and a Dashboard with four figures: total payable, overdue bills, amount due this week and the vendor you owe most, plus charts of bills by status and payables due over the next four weeks. Status flags each bill as Paid, Due soon (within 7 days), Overdue or Unpaid. A Settings tab holds your vendors, categories and currency symbol. No macros.

Catch: No email; the download button appears after a short "preparing your download" wait, and the page carries ads. Entries on the Payment Log don't update the bill, so you type the amount paid on the Bills sheet as well. Status uses TODAY(), so the sample bills (dated mid-2026) all show as overdue until you clear them, and some sample categories are mismatched.

Accounts payable aging tracker

by Drip Capital
ExcelFree

One row per supplier invoice, 40 rows. Enter the invoice date and terms and it fills in the due date, balance, days past due, and which aging bucket the balance sits in (current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90 or over 90 days), with totals and the share of your balance in each bucket. Optional columns work out the dollar saving if you take an early-payment discount in time. A filled-in Example tab shows how it reads.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx download. The as-of date is TODAY(), so the aging moves every day; type a fixed date if you want a month-end snapshot. There's no payment log, just an amount-paid column. Drip Capital is a trade-finance company and the article around the download promotes its services.

Software license & subscription tracker

by ExpirePilot
ExcelFree

The most thorough subscription tracker we found: one row per tool for up to 300 licenses, with seats bought versus seats in use, cost per billing cycle converted to an annual figure, renewal date, notice period and the cancel-by date worked out for you, plus auto-renew and an owner. A Dashboard counts what's expired or renewing in the next 30 and 90 days, shows spend on unused seats and lists the next 10 renewals. Extra tabs cover seat assignments, a renewal log and a plain-English guide to license types.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx download (the "Use in Google Sheets" button downloads the same file, which you upload to Drive). It's a lot of columns for a business with five subscriptions. Exchange rates on the Lists tab are examples, so update them if you pay in other currencies, and delete the 12 sample licenses. ExpirePilot sells renewal-tracking software.

Subscription tracker spreadsheet

by Subskim
ExcelCSVFree

A lighter list for the subscriptions on the company card: service, category, amount, currency, billing cycle, next renewal, trial end date and status, all with dropdowns. It works out the cost per month and per year whether the plan is weekly, monthly, quarterly or yearly, counts days to renewal, and highlights rows a week before they renew and trials three days before they end. A Summary tab totals each currency and counts renewals due in the next 30 days.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx or .csv download. The examples are consumer apps (Spotify, Notion and the like) and some sample renewal dates have already passed. Renewal dates don't roll forward on their own; you move each one on after it renews. There's no notice-period or cancel-by column, and totals stay per currency. Subskim pitches its own free tracker app in the How to use tab.

Petty cash log with cash count check

by AcboBooks
ExcelFree

A 300-line log for the cash box: date, voucher number, paid to, what for, category, cash out, cash in, running balance, whether there's a receipt, and who approved it. The top block shows the float, the balance per the log, how much to top up to restore the float, and over or short once you type in the cash you actually counted. Catch: No sign-up or email; direct .xlsx download. Three sample lines to clear. Nothing is locked, so it's easy to edit and just as easy to type over a formula. AcboBooks sells bookkeeping software and the Instructions tab mentions when to move on from a spreadsheet.

Daily cash report and over/short log

by BookkeepingSheets
ExcelFree

Built for restaurants and small hotels closing out the register. The Daily Cash Report takes the sales and tenders from your POS end-of-day (Z) report, card tips and the processor batch total, paid-outs, and a bill-and-coin count, then works out expected cash, over or short, and the deposit after leaving tomorrow's starting bank. A Cash Log keeps one line per day for the month, and a Month Summary totals sales, tenders, sales tax collected and net over/short. It prints on one landscape page.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx download. Some lines (alcohol, room charges, house accounts) won't apply to a shop, so ignore or relabel them. You copy each day's figures into the Cash Log yourself. It comes filled with a sample month to clear.

Cash drawer count sheet and end-of-day report

by PracticeManager360
ExcelPDFFree

A one-page close-out for a front desk: count each bill and coin and the total adds itself up, then starting float plus cash taken, minus refunds and petty cash paid out, gives what the drawer should hold, with a Balanced, OVER or SHORT flag and the cash to deposit after tomorrow's float. Below that are payments by method and a few counts for the day. A Monthly log tab has one row per day for counted, expected, over/short and deposits.

Catch: No email to download; direct .xlsx and a printable PDF (the email box on the page is a newsletter signup). It was written for medical and dental practices, so lines like HSA/FSA card, visits and no-shows need relabeling for a shop. The monthly log is filled in by hand, not linked to the daily sheet.
