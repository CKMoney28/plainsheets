Do you even need purchase orders?

A purchase order is the buyer's written order, sent before anything ships: what you're buying, how many, at what price and on what terms. It's the mirror image of an invoice. Plenty of very small businesses order by email and skip it, and that's fine. A PO starts paying for itself once you have more than one person ordering, suppliers who deliver in several shipments, or invoices that don't quite match what you agreed. The habit that matters is the check at the end: when the invoice arrives, compare it with the PO and with what was actually received before you pay (accountants call this a three-way match). Give every PO its own number and ask suppliers to quote it on the invoice. To track the stock once it arrives, see our inventory templates; to plan when supplier bills will hit your bank account, see cash flow templates.

Purchase order template

by Vertex42
ExcelFree

The classic printable PO: your company block, vendor and ship-to addresses, requisitioner, ship via, F.O.B. and shipping terms, then item number, description, quantity and unit price with line totals worked out for you. The date fills itself in, and a short note on the sheet explains how to send it as a PDF. The web page also explains each field if terms like F.O.B. are new to you.

Catch: No email; the .xlsx comes from a "your download is ready" page after one extra click. Vertex42's license is for private use, so don't resell or share modified copies. The date cell uses TODAY(), so it changes every time you open the file; type over it with a fixed date before you save a sent order.

Purchase order with vendor list

by Vertex42
ExcelFree

The same PO layout, plus a Vendors tab and a Ship To tab. Type each supplier's name and address once, then pick the vendor and delivery address from a list at the top of the order and the address lines fill in. Saves retyping if you order from the same handful of suppliers every month.

Catch: No email, same one-extra-click download and private-use license as the basic version. Linked from the same Vertex42 page; look for the "Purchase Order with Vendor List" download. Excel only, and like the basic file the date uses TODAY().

Purchase order template (standard and blanket)

by InvoSmith
ExcelFree

Two clean one-page POs: a standard order with a Required By date, and a blanket PO for repeat buying under agreed terms, with a validity window and release number instead. Buyer, vendor, ship-to and bill-to blocks, 20 item lines with automatic amounts and subtotal, and an Editing notes tab that explains how to add or delete rows without breaking the totals.

Catch: No email, sign-up or watermark; both .xlsx files download straight from the page. Tax is a blank cell you type an amount into; it doesn't apply a rate for you. Uses UK spelling ("Authorised by") but no currency symbol, so it works in the US as is.

Purchase order template

by Expeni
ExcelPDFFree

A one-page PO with spots most free templates skip: who requested the purchase, which budget or cost center pays for it, payment terms, currency, and an approval name and signature. Totals take a discount, a tax rate, and shipping, so the PO total is the amount you actually expect to be billed. A How to use tab walks through numbering, approval and matching the invoice later.

Catch: No sign-up; the .xlsx and a printable PDF download straight from the page. Expeni sells purchase-order software and the page and the file's How to use tab pitch it; you don't need it to use the template. Works in Google Sheets if you upload it to Drive.

by FreeBillKit
ExcelFree

The simplest file on this page: PO number, date, deliver-by, your business, vendor, and six item lines that multiply quantity by unit price, with a subtotal and order total. Good if you only raise the occasional PO and want something you can fill in in a minute.

Catch: No signup or watermark; direct .xlsx download. Only six item lines. The page says you can add a tax percentage, but in the file we downloaded tax is a cell you type an amount into, not a rate. No ship-to or approval fields.

Purchase order with receiving log

by The Comfort Stack
ExcelFree

A workbook that keeps what you ordered separate from what turned up. There's a blank PO with ten lines and automatic totals, a filled-in example, and a Receiving Log where each delivery gets its own row: PO and line reference, delivered, accepted, held and rejected quantities, storage location and who checked it. Built with online sellers in mind, so it handles partial deliveries well.

Catch: No signup; direct .xlsx download (a CSV of order lines is offered too). The receiving log is a record only: it doesn't update the PO or your stock count, and there are no tax or currency calculations. It isn't a Shopify import file, despite the Shopify-seller focus.

Purchase order tracking template

by LineNow
ExcelGoogle SheetsFree

A one-row-per-PO-line tracker for keeping tabs on what's still outstanding. Columns keep the original quantity and price next to the current approved ones, then received and cancelled quantities, and work out open quantity and open value. There's room for the next expected date, an exception note, an owner and an approval reference, plus an example tab.

Catch: No signup; direct .xlsx or a Google Sheets "make a copy" link (you'll need to be signed in to Google). It doesn't send reminders or log individual deliveries, so pair it with a receiving log if lines arrive in several shipments. LineNow's site promotes its own software.

Supplier contact & performance tracker

by Spreadsheet Partners
ExcelFree

For when you buy from several suppliers and want to know who's actually reliable. Log each delivery (promised vs actual date), each quality check (received vs rejected), and current prices, and the Scorecard rates every supplier on on-time %, defect % and price against the category average, with weights you set and A/B/C bands. Supplier contacts, payment terms and contract renewal dates sit on the same row.

Catch: No email; direct .xlsx download. The file says it's free for commercial use with no attribution needed. It's the most complex file here, comes full of sample suppliers to clear out, and supplier names must be typed exactly the same on every tab or the counts miss them.

by Spreadsheet Daddy
Google SheetsExcelFree

A plain vendor directory: vendor ID, name, contact person, email, phone, address, what they supply, payment terms, status and notes. One blank tab and one filled with sample vendors so you can see how it looks. Good as the one place your team checks for who to call and what terms you have.

Catch: No email; there's a Google Sheets "make a copy" link (sign in to Google) and a direct .xlsx. No formulas at all, just a formatted list with a status dropdown. Expect ads on the page.

Supplier list template (UK)

by Supplio
ExcelFree

A supplier database with dropdowns for category, status and spend band, an approved-supplier flag, an owner, and a next-review date that turns red when it's overdue. Catch: No email; direct .xlsx download. Built for UK businesses: there's a Companies House number column and spend bands in pounds (£), so US users should relabel or ignore those. Supplio sells supplier-management software and the file mentions it.
