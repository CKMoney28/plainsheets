Budget, budget vs actual or startup costs: which one do you need?

A budget is your plan for the year: what you expect to sell and spend, line by line, usually split by month because few businesses earn the same every month. A budget vs actual sheet puts what really happened next to the plan, so you can see which lines are off and by how much; look at it every month, not just in January. A driver-based budget builds the plan from the numbers you control, such as units, prices and headcount, so changing one assumption updates everything. A startup cost worksheet adds up what you need before you open, plus a cash cushion for the first months, and compares it with the money you have. Check which way each template shows a variance: some show a positive number when you beat the plan, others when actual is simply higher, which for a cost means you overspent. These are planning tools, not tax or financial advice; how startup costs are deducted and which taxes to budget for depend on your situation, so check with your accountant. Small business budget with monthly variance flags

by Bookkeeping Sheets
ExcelGoogle SheetsFree

Up to 50 budget lines, each put in a group (revenue, cost of goods sold, operating expense, other income or other expense) with a budget for each of the 12 months. Type your actuals from your P&L each month, set the month you're reporting on, and it shows the month and year-to-date variance in dollars and percent, marks each line favorable or unfavorable, and flags the lines that miss by more than both of your limits (say 10% and $500). A one-page report adds a full-year forecast, the five worst misses and a chart. Your fiscal year can start in any month.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx (Excel, Google Sheets or LibreOffice). The sample is a coffee shop, so clear the yellow cells before you start. The forecast simply assumes the rest of the year goes to budget. Actuals are monthly totals you type in, not a transaction log.

Small business budget with a dashboard

by Business Tools Lab
ExcelFree

One sheet with 22 ready-made categories (two revenue lines, four cost-of-goods lines and 16 operating expenses such as rent, payroll, software and card fees), with a budget, actual and variance column for every month and a total for the year. The dashboard adds up revenue, cost of goods sold, gross profit, operating expenses and net profit for budget against actual, and shows budget and actual net profit month by month. It starts blank.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx. For Google Sheets you upload it. The two sheets show variance in opposite ways for costs: on the monthly sheet a positive number means you spent less than planned, on the dashboard it means you spent more. Adding a category means editing the dashboard formulas, and the Setup sheet is a reference list only.

Business budget: actual vs budget for the year

by Vertex42
ExcelFree

A one-year budget laid out like a profit and loss statement, with a sheet for service businesses and one for businesses that sell goods (with cost of goods sold and gross profit). Fill in budget and actual for each line, from accounting and legal, advertising and insurance to rent, wages and web hosting, and it works out the difference, net income before taxes and net income. A third version on the same page shows each line as a percentage of total sales.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx, Excel only. Private-use license. Annual totals only; for months see the 12-month version below. The difference is actual minus budget on every line, so a positive figure on a cost means you overspent. The header still reads 2022 Budget; type your own year.

Budget template with monthly budget and actual columns

by business.gov.au
ExcelFree

The Australian government's simple business budget: up to 11 income lines and 14 expense lines, a budget and an actual column for each month, totals and net income, and a summary at the bottom showing budget, actual and the difference for every month. A notes sheet explains each step. Plain and blank, with nothing to switch off.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx. Written for Australia: the months run July to June (retype the headings for a January start) and it assumes amounts include GST, which you can ignore elsewhere. The difference is budget minus actual. No yearly total column, and the row labels are placeholders like Income 1.

Driver-based annual budget (units, prices and headcount)

by Covirage
ExcelFree

For a business with staff and several products. Enter each product's yearly units, price and cost-of-goods percentage, a monthly spread (weights that add up to 100%), each role's headcount, salary and start month plus a benefits and payroll-tax rate, and other costs spread evenly or by season. It builds the budget P&L by quarter and month and runs five checks that should all show 0. A budget-vs-actual sheet compares one month at a time.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx (Excel 2016 or later; uploads to Google Sheets). The sample is a $4.2 million company set for 2027, so it's more than a one-person shop needs. The budget-vs-actual sheet shows one month, not year to date. Months are fixed January to December. Covirage sells a data analysis tool you don't need.

Startup budget: funding vs costs, budget and actual

by Smartsheet
ExcelGoogle SheetsFree

Funding from owners, loans and other sources sits next to pre-launch costs in five groups (administrative, location and office, marketing, pre-launch hiring and other, with a contingency line). Each line has a budget, an actual, the over/under amount and a due date, and an overview at the top shows funding less expenses for budget and actual. Good for tracking the money going out in the months before you open.

Catch: No sign-up; direct .xlsx plus a Google Sheets copy link. Over/under runs actual minus budget for funding but budget minus actual for costs, so read the signs carefully. No monthly running costs or cash reserve; pair it with Vertex42's or BusinessPlanDownload's sheet for that. Create-in-Smartsheet banner and disclaimer sheet.
