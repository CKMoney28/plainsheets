Quote or estimate: which do you need?

An estimate is your best guess at the price before you know everything, for example before you open up a wall. A quote is a firm price for a defined scope, often with an expiry date and a signature line. Many trades send a rough estimate first and a quote once the scope is clear. Whichever you send, write down what's not included, set a valid-until date so material price changes don't eat your margin, and keep your costs and markup on a separate tab or file so the customer only sees the price. Remember that markup and margin aren't the same: a 25% markup on cost is a 20% margin on the price. Whether an accepted quote is binding depends on where you work, so this is general information, not legal advice.
